# Chapter 6 — Conclusion and Future Work

Nothing reported in this chapter is externally validated, and under this project's governance nothing in it can be: the one corpus able to confer external validation was locked, scored exactly once, and is spent (section 3.9). Everything here is the specified successor of section 1.7, resting on development-set evidence alone, and it shares no table with a validated figure. The chapter opens with the claim the whole thesis has been assembling, stated as one claim: every costly decision in this work lowered a number.

- Participant grouping confined every recording from one person to a single fold, forgoing the easier evaluation much of this literature uses (section 3.6).
- Age and sex matching set aside every Pittsburgh recording that could not be pair-matched, after age alone was measured to classify that corpus at 0.707 (section 5.2).
- The corpus-compatibility rule declined a pooling measured to manufacture performance, two studies' healthy speakers being separable at 0.930 (section 3.2.1).
- The lock permanently excluded a known improvement — pause and timing measures, among the strongest predictors in this literature, implemented and deliberately left unwired (section 6.2).
- The age-residualisation technique that scored 0.853 on development data was discarded when age proved recoverable from the supposedly age-free features at R² = 0.994 (section 5.8).
- The external corpus could be spent once, and was (section 3.9).
- Fifteen claims were tested, did not survive their pre-registered criteria or their motivating hopes, and are reported in full rather than dropped (section 5.8).

Two disciplines keep that list from congratulating itself. First, this work tested its own favourite version of the argument and declines it: ungrouped folds inflate nothing on this model class — −0.0025 despite 68.4% participant exposure (section 3.6.1) — so no claim is made that published figures are inflated by fold leakage. Second, section 5.1.1 concedes that the whole modelling apparatus buys about 4.5 points of AUC over one hand-countable quantity. A project that would not claim an advantage it could not measure has earned the one it can: the development figure of 0.7550 was produced under every constraint above at once. It is not a modest result. It is an expensive one. The instrument that produced it extracts 117 speech and language measures across five families. Its externally evaluated English screening model uses a frozen 64-feature transcript-derived subset of linguistic, information-content and discourse-semantic measures; 53 acoustic measures support a separate language-independent pathway. Sections 6.1 and 6.2 state what was learned and what limits it; sections 6.3 to 6.5 specify what should be built next; section 6.6 closes.

## 6.1 Conclusions

The contributions are listed with the methodological ones first, because they are exactly derived, usable by other groups, and independent of whether this particular model is any good. Appendix F grades every component of the system on a levels-of-evidence table; the points below state the conclusions that grading supports, each with its evidential status in the same breath.

1. **Control-referenced thresholding, with its exact precision result — the principal methodological contribution (sections 5.16 to 5.18).** A screening threshold fixed on one population does not transport, and every obvious re-fixing rule reads the unlabelled marginal score distribution, which makes it arithmetically a referral-rate rule that moves with local prevalence — mean-matching collapses to 1.1% specificity on one transfer for exactly this reason (section 5.17). The rule that transports by construction sets the threshold at a fixed percentile of the local healthy-control distribution: specificity becomes a design parameter, sensitivity the reported variable, and the argument holds independently of any cohort — the measured 79.8 / 79.9 / 77.8 across three cohorts illustrate the construction behaving as derived (section 5.18). The companion result converts the rule into a sample-size calculation: a threshold at the k-th order statistic of n local controls has prospective specificity following a Beta(k, n+1−k) law, so 20 controls buy [0.56, 0.91], about 59 buy ±10 points, about 108 buy ±7.5. The rule needs healthy speakers and no patients, inverting the deployment economics of a low-resource setting. Its status travels with it: post hoc on the frozen model, not pre-registered, not deployed — the operating point remains 0.367 and the primary external result remains the one reported at it.
2. **A screening system validated across corpora with a locked, one-shot external test, and the governance record around it** — the contamination identified by external review, the lock that answered it, the retraining, the single scoring at a threshold fixed in advance, and the exposure history reported rather than smoothed (section 3.9).
3. **Corpus-compatibility testing as a named, cheap pre-pooling check.** Before pooling two corpora, test whether a classifier can separate their healthy speakers; this project measured 0.930 between two healthy groups, traced it to transcription convention, and derived the rule that corpora are combined only when each contributes both classes (section 3.2.1). The answer is model-dependent — a published counter-case pooled the same corpora successfully under a contextual representation (section 2.3) — which is exactly why the test must be run per project; it cannot be assumed in either direction.
4. **An Arabic contribution in three parts — an instrument for the dementia target, a documented resource gap, and a corpus specification** (sections 6.4 and 6.5). The adaptation alone would overstate it; the gap alone would undersell it.
5. **A refusal architecture, enforced in code.** The system declines to produce a result for audio below the level at which pause extraction was measured to fail, for a stimulus other than the calibrated one, for an Arabic session — no validated Arabic screening score exists — and for any use outside the model card's declared scope (sections 3.8 and 4.5). Encoding a competence boundary and enforcing it at runtime is rare in this literature, and it is a biomedical-engineering contribution rather than a machine-learning one.
6. **An exploratory overlearned-memory probe adapted to the deployment culture** — Qur'anic recitation, scored against the surah actually recited, framed throughout as exploratory, with six documented confounders and no claimed validity (section 4.2).
7. **A deployable, quality-gated assessment platform that degrades to paper** (Chapter 4): a screening score where computer, model and recogniser are present; an indicator profile without the model; a hand-countable checklist without any device.
8. **Fifteen documented negative results in one register** (section 5.8): three changed a design decision, four bounded what is reachable, two removed arguments this thesis would otherwise have made, two settled a specification question, one produced the project's strongest positive finding by failing, one redrew the boundary of a contribution before it was written in, and the final two each bounded a component's claim.
9. **A pre-registration practice with a stated failure mode and a stated remedy.** Four defects arose in seventeen registered runs, every one in something the registration did not think it needed to state — a tokenisation rule, a denominator's interpretability, a label rule, and a comparison whose two sides were estimated by different procedures (section 3.10). The remedy is a rule any project can adopt: register a sanity gate on every derived quantity an analysis depends on, including its labels. The failure mode is wider than registrations, and its general form is this project's most transferable lesson: every defect this work found in its own analysis, artwork and deployment was silent — a tokenisation filter differing from the committed extractor, a label rule taking the first row in file order rather than the first visit, bootstrap intervals resampled over recordings and so 1.09 to 1.31 times too narrow, a stimulus missing two of the twenty-three units scored against it, nine of sixty-four features silently median-imputed on every machine the one-click installer configured. Not one raised an error; each returned a value of the expected type and plausible magnitude, because each component met its contract — return a number — while the number's meaning had changed. The one defect that announced itself, an import of a missing symbol, cost about a minute; every silent one survived for days to weeks. A component whose contract is to return a value cannot report that the value has stopped meaning what it meant: something outside it must — the pre-registered sanity gates, the mechanical document checks, and a deployed report that names its own substitutions (sections 3.10 and 4.5.1).

### 6.1.1 What The Modelling Apparatus Is Worth

The calibrated ensemble over 64 engineered features is the expensive part of this system: it needs a computer, cannot run on paper, and is the component a low-resource deployment would most like to drop. What it buys over counting one quantity was measured on both targets with both sides estimated alike — the comparator being the best single feature selected inside each training fold, never the best feature chosen with the whole dataset in view (section 5.28). On the development target the ensemble reaches 0.7550 against a fairly estimated single-feature comparator at 0.7096, a gain of +0.0455; on mild-impairment discourse, 0.6379 against 0.6018, a gain of +0.0361. Roughly four points of AUC on both targets — and there is no four points versus nothing: the earlier appearance that the apparatus bought nothing on discourse came entirely from a comparator estimated differently from the model (section 5.28).

The asymmetry that does exist concerns the stability of an instruction, not the size of a gain. On picture description the best single feature is the information-unit total in five folds of five, with selection optimism exactly 0.0000, so the paper instruction — count the information units — is stable and transferable. On discourse no measure is stably best — word count wins three folds of five, and the informative measures are largely re-expressions of output volume — so a field worker could not reliably be told what to count. That produces a reversal: section 5.25 establishes that for mild impairment the discourse genre carries the signal and the picture task performs at chance, while the paper fallback is more dependable on the picture than on discourse. The picture-description path is simultaneously the weaker task and the stronger fallback, and the resolution, stated in Table (6.1), is a deployment finding in its own right — the right task depends on whether the computer is present.

**Table (6.1) : The task choice depends on whether the computer is present**

| | With the software | Without it, on paper |
|---|---|---|
| Which task | connected discourse | picture description |
| Why | the genre effect: +0.0788 [0.0042, 0.1579] for two discourse tasks over three picture tasks, on the target where early detection changes management (section 5.25) | one countable quantity, selected in five folds of five, keeps the field instruction stable; on discourse no measure is reliably best (section 5.28) |
| What it costs | a device, an extractor and the frozen model | about 4.5 points of AUC against the deployed ensemble (section 5.1.1) |

A deployment that expects to lose the computer some of the time should not drop the discourse tasks and keep the picture: it should administer both, score discourse when it can, and fall back to counting information units on the picture when it cannot — which is what the pilot protocol of Appendix B implements. Beside the argument that opened this chapter, this is the same claim measured on the cheapest axis: the apparatus is worth about four points, and everything else this thesis contains — the lock, the negative-result register, the pre-registration discipline, the external validation — is worth more than that (section 6.6).

## 6.2 Limitations

At the deployed operating point the instrument is wrong about roughly one person in three: accuracy is about 67% on development data and 34 of 53 on the locked external set. The AUC of 0.7550 must not be read as wrong about one in four — AUC is a pairwise ranking statistic, and the correct reading is that, given one impaired and one healthy recording, the model ranks the pair correctly about three times in four.

The external specificity of 33.3% is the headline weakness, and its diagnosed form says more than its raw form. The threshold was not inefficient: at the sensitivity it reached, it captured essentially all the specificity the curve permitted — the median recovery fraction across the eighty transported-threshold cells is 0.981 (section 5.16) — but it landed at 96.2% sensitivity where the design intent was near 75%, spending specificity on sensitivity nobody asked for. The eighteen misclassified external controls had genuinely described the picture less well, 11.5 information units against 14.9, so no threshold rule could have separated them and the model was not malfunctioning (section 5.19). A threshold referenced to local healthy controls delivers specificity to target in all three cohorts (section 5.18), which identifies the remedy without claiming it. What remains a limitation after the diagnosis, because it does: the deployed system still carries 0.367 and therefore still has 33.3% specificity on that corpus; the remedy is analysed, not implemented, and was chosen after seeing the data; and the task-administration explanation for the false positives is a hypothesis, confounded with an 8.4-year age difference in a sample of 27, and untested.

The deployed picture-description architecture performs weakly for mild impairment, and no threshold rule can change that: at the deployed model's discrimination on that cohort the specificity ceiling at 75% sensitivity is 0.376 (section 5.15), and the cohort cannot be learned from its own recordings at all (section 5.12.1). Within the available multi-task corpus, connected discourse is the more promising elicitation genre — a genre the deployed model was never fitted on (section 5.25). The instrument is not validated in Libya and has never been used with a real patient; the Arabic engine's word lists are drafted against Modern Standard Arabic and unvalidated against Libyan dialect. The external test set's exposure is stated in the canonical form: the corpus was excluded from the training data of the final model and from every modelling decision after the lock. Before the lock, five exploratory scorings occurred, one of which informed the decision to include Delaware in the development pool. That one architectural decision — the training pool's composition — was made with knowledge of a score from that corpus, so 0.8533 validates the frozen English screening model on data absent from its training but is not free of that one prior exposure (section 3.9).

The picture the deployed system shows is not the picture the model was fitted on. Two of the 23 scored units were missing from the drawn scene until the audit of section 4.3.1 found them, and the residual mismatch that cannot be corrected graded DISPLACEMENT-MATERIAL: if the substituted picture costs a speaker even one information unit, 13.1% of control recordings cross the screening threshold (section 4.3.1). Whether it costs real speakers anything is unknown and cannot be known without Libyan data. This is the largest single untested assumption standing between the reported external figure and the deployed instrument — larger than the threshold-transfer problem that sits beside it.

The measured sex disparity leads the instrument's equity limitations: a specificity failure with a measured mechanism (section 5.2.1) that no threshold or rescaling can repair, as the invariance result of section 3.11 proves. It is carried into section 6.5 as a design requirement on the successor rather than patched by a step that provably cannot patch it.

The cost of the lock is named beside its benefit. Freezing the model bought a one-shot external evaluation; it also permanently excluded a known improvement. A pause-and-timing feature module is implemented, tested and deliberately unwired — kept outside the deployed package, in `future_work/pause_features.py`, because wiring it would create a model the external corpus never validated — while pause and timing measures are among the strongest reported predictors in this literature and the deployed 64 features contain none of them; the robustness analysis makes the same point from the other side, since pause measures are the first thing background noise destroys (section 5.9). The deployed model is knowingly not the best model this feature work could produce, and correcting that requires a fourth corpus carrying both classes, because the existing external test is spent.

Finally, the recitation probe's motivating dissociation — overlearned material preserved while episodic memory fails — has no data behind it, and its nearest English analogue is both imperfect and unsupportive: the story retell of section 5.25 is an immediate episodic recall task resting on a semantic scaffold rather than rote verbatim production, and to the extent it bears on the question at all it points the other way, separating the groups better than spontaneous description does rather than worse. The probe remains exploratory, and nothing in this thesis treats it otherwise.

## 6.3 Future Work

The future work follows from the limitations, and its first sentence is a boundary rather than a plan. The same properties that make the instrument administrable in a Libyan home without a clinic — no network, no specialist, a paper fallback — make it administrable in a displacement or post-disaster setting, where cognitive screening is least available and cognitive harm is most concentrated; whether it is *useful* there is entirely untested and would require a study designed for that setting.

The principal future work is the Libyan study, and its pathway is reported at exactly the strength it has. Clinical validation has not yet been performed. A pathway toward prospective Libyan validation has been established through engagement with a national patient organisation. That engagement is stakeholder engagement, not clinical validation: it is confined to recruitment feasibility and cultural acceptability, the organisation has not reviewed, assessed or endorsed the instrument, it has provided no clinical input, and no patient data has been involved. The reference standard for the proposed study requires clinician-confirmed diagnosis; contact has been initiated and no arrangement is in place. Securing a named clinician willing to confirm diagnoses is the first step of the study, and it is not yet done; the protocol's reference-standard section is therefore written as what would be required — the Arabic Montreal Cognitive Assessment administered blind to the system's output — rather than as an arrangement.

The feasibility pilot is submitted as a completed, ethics-ready protocol (Appendix B), and its endpoint structure is fixed in advance: the eventual study's powered primary endpoint is dementia against healthy controls, with a pre-specified mild-impairment arm that is explicitly underpowered and descriptive only, reported with no test statistic — the rule of section 1.7 applied to a study design instead of a table. What the pilot delivers is deliberately modest: battery feasibility with elderly Libyan participants, the first Libyan healthy normative distributions, validation of the Arabic word lists against dialect actually spoken, a provisional control-referenced threshold reported at its exact interval width and labelled provisional, the elicitation-administration measurements no existing corpus records, and the pre-registered stimulus-equivalence check of the substituted picture (section 4.3.1). It cannot test discrimination — at 20 per group it detects only a true AUC of 0.748 or larger, above every effect this project has measured — and the protocol says so in its own words. One audit is also left to this stage: the courtyard and market scenes have not been audited against their own content inventories as the kitchen scene was, and the system continues to withhold any probability for them until they are (section 4.5).

For the model itself, the staged validation design below is this project's own prescription for what it could not do, and it follows from the measured transport failures of sections 5.13 and 5.14 — one global threshold does not transport, and one global recalibration cannot repair it, because the development cohorts are miscalibrated in opposite directions. The stages are strict and ordered.

1. A development pool for fitting and feature decisions, as now.
2. A dedicated calibration cohort, used openly and repeatedly to set the operating threshold and the recalibration map, and declared as spent. The locked corpus is the natural candidate: its scores are already on the record, so re-designating it costs nothing that has not already been paid — and what it may never again be called is an external test set.
3. A freeze of everything — model, threshold, calibration map, feature set — recorded with a timestamp, as at the lock.
4. One evaluation on a corpus never touched, pre-registered, single execution, reported whatever it says.

Candidate corpora for the fourth stage were checked for provenance, and the checking is part of the record.

- **TAUKADIAL** — 169 participants and 507 recordings of picture description in Mandarin and English, targeting mild impairment, distributed through the same archive membership as this project's corpora; its English arm even uses the standard kitchen-scene stimulus. It is not clean for this project until checked: that arm is a University of Delaware community cohort recruited under the same programme as the Delaware corpus in this project's training pool, so speaker overlap is plausible and unexcluded. Before it could serve, its English participant identifiers would have to be checked against the 291 Delaware training participants and any overlap removed — or the evaluation restricted to the Mandarin arm, a different language and therefore a different claim [1].
- **The Framingham Heart Study voice archive** — digitally recorded neuropsychological examinations linked to longitudinal, clinically adjudicated dementia outcomes, independent of DementiaBank, and already used for dementia detection at the scale of over a thousand recordings. The demanding half is practical: the recordings are long two-speaker examiner-and-participant sessions, so the pipeline would need a diarisation stage it does not have, and access runs through a formal application, institutional approval and committee review [2].
- **ADReSS and ADReSSo are excluded**, and the reason is stated because an examiner will ask why the obvious benchmarks were skipped. ADReSS is, in its organisers' words, "a curated subset of DementiaBank's Pitt Corpus, matched for age and gender": evaluating a model trained on all 548 Pittsburgh recordings against it would be direct test-set contamination, and ADReSSo draws on the same pool with likely speaker-level overlap. Declining the benchmark is a governance point in this project's favour, not an omission [3].

Three further directions close the list. Pretrained speech and text representations remain the principal untested modelling direction; they were unattemptable in the development environment and would forfeit the paper fallback and the explainable indicator profile if adopted wholesale (section 5.1.2). Per-deployment threshold derivation on local healthy controls, with the exact interval supplying its sample size, is the operational form of the first contribution (section 5.18). And the pause-and-timing module is wired in only after a fourth both-class corpus exists to validate the enlarged model, for the reason section 6.2 gives.

### 6.3.1 The Instrument A Libyan Clinic Would Deploy First

Three instruments exist in this work, and a Libyan clinic could deploy exactly one of them in the near term; the thesis picks it rather than describing three candidates and choosing between none. Table (6.2) states the three paths and what each requires.

**Table (6.2) : The three Arabic deployment paths and what each requires**

| Path | What it needs to run in an Arabic clinic | Can it run now |
|---|---|---|
| The English linguistic model | Arabic speech recognition that survives dialect; Arabic part-of-speech tagging; Arabic normative ranges, which do not exist | No |
| The Arabic linguistic engine | a validated threshold, which needs the corpus documented as missing in section 6.4 | No |
| The language-independent acoustic model | audio | Yes |

The acoustic model needs no transcription, no tagger, no lexicon and no normative ranges, and its cost relative to the linguistic instrument is measured: 0.708 against 0.755 on the matched English comparison, about five points of AUC. Its provenance is stated precisely, because the imprecise version understates it: the original training script is lost, but the configuration was recovered by introspection of the deployed artifact, the protocol was confirmed by a pre-registered signature test, and the embedded figure reproduces from a fresh fit run by committed code, to within 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ — a stronger position than possessing the original script, because it has been independently verified rather than merely trusted. The substantive caveat belongs in the same paragraph as the recommendation: 0.708 was measured on a matched English subset of 373 recordings, its transfer to Arabic is exactly as unvalidated as everything else in section 6.4, and the one measured Arabic figure — 0.622 [0.378, 0.857] on the 24-speaker pilot, an interval spanning chance, with seven controls — comes from this same acoustic model. The strategic sentence this thesis has earned is short: two instruments were built, and the weaker one is the deployable one. What changes that is an Arabic corpus meeting the specification of section 6.5, at which point the linguistic instrument becomes available and the acoustic one becomes its fallback rather than its front line.

## 6.4 The Arabic Contribution

The Arabic contribution is stated as what it is — an instrument for the dementia target, a documented resource gap, and a corpus specification — because the English construct validation changed what may be claimed, and the change is reported at full strength. The referential deficit index discriminates in a dementia cohort: 0.596 [0.541, 0.652] on Pittsburgh, improving to 0.625 [0.562, 0.690] on the age- and sex-matched subset — within the literature identified by this search, the first computation of the construct on the speech of diagnosed patients in any language (section 5.10.1). On mild impairment it sits at chance on all five tasks of the one corpus permitting the test, and so does the English pronoun marker it replaces (section 5.27). The failure is therefore in the construct, not the implementation and not the language: the index tracks the marker it stands in for on both targets — somewhat lossy on dementia, equally uninformative on mild impairment. On this English evidence, target and stage are the dominant explanation: the index separates at the dementia stage and not at the mild-impairment stage, and nothing measured here supports it as an early marker in any language.

The pro-drop argument survives unchanged, and it is restated so that the two conclusions are not confused. That argument concerns portability: Arabic normally omits the subject pronoun because the verb carries person, number and gender, so pronoun counts sit on a different baseline for grammatical rather than clinical reasons, and the English pronoun-overuse marker cannot be carried across (section 3.4). Nothing measured here touches that. The referential deficit index remains the correct replacement for it; what changed is not whether the replacement works but what it is for. The cost is this: mild impairment is where early detection changes management and is the future study's most valuable target, and the Arabic component as designed cannot address it. Making it a mild-impairment instrument requires an Arabic lemmatiser, because morphological richness confounds surface lexical-diversity measures; an Arabic dependency parser; and Arabic embeddings — all running offline on the target hardware. That is a substantially different engineering problem from the one this project solved, and naming its three components exactly is worth more than claiming coverage the measurement does not support (section 6.5).

What the measurement saved exceeds what it cost. It arrived before 240 Libyan participants were recruited against a marker that cannot detect what the study most needs to screen for — and had the Arabic study run to the earlier specification, a single-task picture-description corpus sized for the referential index, it would have returned a correctly powered null that was uninterpretable, because a single-task, single-language study cannot distinguish a wrong marker from a wrong task from a wrong implementation. The English probes separate all three: the cross-task probe shows the marker silent across five tasks in English, which eliminates the language and isolates the construct (section 5.27), and the task-count analysis establishes genre as a separate, real axis (section 5.25). The finding did not merely restrict a claim; it changed what the corpus must contain — both genres from every speaker — so that the next investigator is never left holding the same unanswerable question.

The resource gap is documented rather than asserted, with its scope stated first: at the time of the search, no publicly available corpus could be identified that pairs cognitively impaired and cognitively healthy Arabic speakers on a connected-speech task with transcripts. Each qualifier does work: clinical archives surely hold such recordings — the claim concerns what a researcher can obtain; word-list, naming and repetition batteries do not elicit the referential behaviour the index measures; and audio without transcripts supports no lexical or syntactic measurement, since Arabic speech recognition is not dependable on dialectal elderly speech. The evidence is a documented search, not a systematic review, and it brackets the gap from both sides. DementiaBank, the field's canonical repository and the source of this project's corpora, lists English, German, Greek, Korean, Mandarin, Spanish and Taiwanese sections and no Arabic (talkbank.org/dementia/access, retrieved during the search). A 2024 survey of the field names twelve datasets, none of them Arabic [2]. The newest benchmark for early cognitive impairment is British English only, named by its authors as a limitation [4]. The one Arabic dataset with both classes — the 24-speaker collection this project's acoustic pilot used — elicits Montreal Cognitive Assessment subtest responses, word lists rather than connected speech [5]. A systematic review of cognitive assessment for Arabic speakers, 154 studies, found the validated instruments to be structured batteries and questionnaires, with no connected-speech instrument among them [6]. And Arabic connected speech exists in quantity in speech-recognition corpora that carry no clinical labels of any kind. Data with both classes and no connected speech; connected speech with no labels; never the join. A search returning both halves and never the intersection is far stronger evidence of a missing join than a search returning nothing — and the claim is falsifiable: one counterexample retires it, and that would be good news.

Against that gap, the contribution is the three parts named above: a working Arabic engine that computes the replacement marker, its evidential status stated exactly — implemented, verified on constructed examples, probed for construct validity in English only, never computed on the speech of an Arabic-speaking patient; the documented absence, stated at the strength the search supports and no more; and the specification of section 6.5, which is the part another group could act on tomorrow.

## 6.5 The Corpus Specification

What the successor corpus must contain is specified in full in Appendix I, derived entirely from this project's own measurements; this section states what the specification requires and what it is for. It is led by genre rather than by named tasks — connected discourse for the mild-impairment target, picture description retained for the dementia target — and it requires both genres from every participant, because a single-genre corpus cannot separate a marker failure from a task failure, the confusion section 6.4 records this project as having narrowly avoided. It fixes elicitation, because word count is the strongest single mild-impairment measure on discourse and under variable administration it measures the examiner; it requires transcription that preserves the dialectal demonstratives and vague nouns the dementia measurement is made of; it splits the marker families by target, labelling the referential index as the dementia-stage measure it is; and it requires the healthy arm to recruit both sexes in numbers sufficient for sex-specific reference ranges, the design requirement carried from section 5.2.1. Its sample sizes come from the measured effects: 141 per group for the dementia referential effect, 130 for the mild-impairment discourse effect powered on the conservative end of its range, 108 healthy controls for a threshold precise to ±7.5 points, and a recommended 120 impaired with 120 healthy — with an earlier recommendation that the mild-impairment arm was the cheaper one withdrawn in place, since, powered on the conservative end, the two arms cost essentially the same and the choice between targets is scientific rather than budgetary. The specification is the actionable third of the Arabic contribution: the part another group could take up tomorrow, with the pilot of Appendix B as its first increment.

## 6.6 The Closing Claim

The frozen English screening model reached external AUC 0.8533 [0.7371, 0.9458] on data absent from its training, at a threshold fixed in advance, scored once, under the exposure history of section 3.9 — sensitivity 96.2%, specificity 33.3%. Subsequent threshold-transport analysis recovered external specificity to 77.8% by control-referenced thresholding — a post-hoc methodological contribution, not preregistered, not deployed, and requiring prospective validation. The apparatus that produces it — sixty-four engineered features, a calibrated ensemble, the whole modelled part of the system — is worth about four points of AUC over counting one quantity by hand, measured the same way on both targets (section 6.1.1). That is a real gain, and a small one.

> **The entire modelling apparatus is worth about four points of AUC, and everything else this thesis contains is worth more than that.**

The lock that made one external number mean something, and cost three results to enforce. The fifteen negative results, three of which changed a design decision and two of which removed arguments this thesis would otherwise have made. The pre-registration discipline, with its four disclosed failures and the working rules derived from them. The reconciliation of every figure that has ever held more than one value. The measurement that the Arabic instrument addresses dementia rather than the target the future study most needs — made before two hundred and forty people were recruited against it. None of that is accuracy, and all of it is what makes the accuracy readable. A number without those things is the field's present situation: more than a decade of reported performance above ninety per cent, a minority of studies reporting statistical significance, and no tool in routine clinical use (section 2.3). This thesis is four points of modelling and a great deal of knowing what the four points mean.
