# Appendix I — The Corpus Specification

This appendix is the full specification of the Arabic corpus whose absence section 6.4 documents: what such a corpus must contain for its results to be interpretable, with every figure derived from this project's own measurements rather than asserted. Its evidential sources are the task-count and genre analyses (`results/reconstruction/task_count_curve.json`, `results/reconstruction/feature_by_task_auc.json`), the minimal-probe and selection-optimism measurements (`results/reconstruction/minimal_probe.json`, `results/reconstruction/selection_optimism.json`), and the threshold-precision result (`results/reconstruction/control_threshold_precision.json`). Chapter 6 section 6.5 carries the summary; the pilot protocol of Appendix B is this specification's first increment.

## I.1 The Claim That Transfers

The specification is led by the claim that transfers. A corpus built from scratch cannot inherit an instruction to use a particular named task; it can inherit a claim about what kind of task carries the signal. On the one corpus administering five tasks to both classes, the two connected-discourse tasks occupy the top two places for mild impairment and the three picture-description tasks the bottom three, with the paired battery contrast excluding zero — +0.0788 [0.0042, 0.1579] for two discourse tasks over three picture tasks (section 5.25). The genre level is the right level because it survives translation, cultural substitution and the replacement of every individual stimulus — exactly what building a corpus in another language requires. The mechanism makes it plausible rather than merely observed: a picture bounds what there is to say, so output volume carries little information once the depicted content is named, while a narrative or a procedure must be generated and sustained, so volume becomes a measure of what the speaker can still produce — word count moves from near-uninformative on picture description to the strongest single mild-impairment measure on discourse (section 5.27). The corpus therefore collects both genres from every participant: the picture task serves the dementia target and the referential index, the discourse tasks serve mild impairment, and a single-genre corpus could not separate a marker failure from a task failure — the confusion section 6.4 records this project as having narrowly avoided.

## I.2 The Battery Instantiated For Libya


The battery instantiates the genres for Libya. Story recall uses an examiner-read story retold immediately: supplying the narrative in the session equalises prior familiarity by construction, which makes it the cleaner instrument psychometrically as well as the culturally safe one, and an established scoring tradition exists to translate against (section 4.2). The source corpus's story-retell task is deliberately not translated, because its own protocol requires prior familiarity with the story — a scaffold Libyan participants cannot be assumed to share. Procedural discourse asks for tea-making, chosen because essentially every participant has performed it hundreds of times, so failure reflects discourse organisation rather than unfamiliarity — and it is the one task a paper administration preserves intact. Picture description uses this project's own stimuli with the frozen key, retained for the dementia target and for continuity with the validated English instrument (section 4.3).

## I.3 Protocol Requirements

Three protocol requirements are not optional. Elicitation is fixed — a verbatim prompt, an enforced maximum duration, a stated re-prompt rule, with elicitation time and probe count recorded per session — because word count is the strongest single mild-impairment measure on discourse, and under variable administration it measures the examiner rather than the speaker; the eighteen thin descriptions behind the external specificity failure are the cautionary case (sections 5.19 and 5.27). Transcription is orthographic and preserves dialectal forms: demonstratives and vague nouns are the dementia measurement, so a convention that normalises them to Modern Standard Arabic destroys the signal; utterance boundaries, pause locations, repetitions and reformulations are marked; and existential and complementiser uses of double-duty items are separated where possible — Arabic هناك does the same double duty that put the naive English implementation at chance on one cohort (section 5.10.1). Metadata records age, sex, years of education, literacy, dialect region, the reference-standard diagnosis with the instrument that made it, and the elicitation measurements above; dialects are reported and never pooled untested, under the corpus-compatibility rule of section 6.1.

## I.4 Marker Families By Target And The Minimal Probe


The marker families divide by target, and the division is the measurement's consequence. For mild impairment on discourse the informative families are output volume, computable today and needing only the fixed elicitation; lexical richness, needing an Arabic lemmatiser; semantic coherence, needing offline Arabic embeddings; syntactic complexity, needing an Arabic dependency parser, though mean sentence length is computable now; and marked disfluency, which is a transcription requirement. For dementia, the referential deficit index is implemented today — and is labelled, wherever it is reported, as a dementia-stage measure. A minimal probe is buildable now with no missing component: total words across the two discourse tasks under fixed elicitation, countable from a transcript with no software. Its expected value, from the English evidence, lies between roughly 0.60 and 0.647 — the upper end selection-inflated, the conservative end the one powered on — and its threshold is always derived from local hand-scored controls, never imported: hand counts run about 1.5 units above the software's (section 5.5.1), and Arabic morphology compresses word counts relative to English, so an imported cut-off would be wrong twice over. The pilot registers the probe as a descriptive secondary outcome, not a test (section 5.28).

## I.5 Sample Sizes And The Recorded Rulings

Table (I.1) turns the project's own measured effects into the study the specification requires, computed by the standard method for a difference in AUC at 80% power and two-sided α = 0.05 [1].

**Table (I.1) : Sample sizes for the Libyan corpus, derived from this project's measured effects**

| Purpose | Powered on | n per group |
|---|---|---|
| Dementia, referential index | true AUC 0.596 | 141 |
| Mild impairment, discourse word count, conservative end | true AUC 0.600 | 130 |
| Mild impairment, selection-inflated upper end | true AUC 0.642 | 65 — not to be powered on |
| Control arm, threshold precise to ±7.5 points | exact Beta interval | 108 healthy |
| Recommended | — | 120 impaired and 120 healthy |

Two rulings on the table are recorded so they are not rediscovered. An earlier recommendation that the mild-impairment arm was the cheaper one is withdrawn: it rested on the selection-inflated end of the range, and powered on the conservative end the two arms cost essentially the same — about 130 against 141 per group — so the choice between targets is scientific rather than budgetary. And the 20-per-group pilot detects only a true AUC of 0.748 or larger, above every effect this project has measured, so it tests neither arm and says so by design; the descriptive endpoint structure of section 6.3 is the consequence. One further requirement is carried from section 5.2.1: the healthy arm recruits both sexes in numbers sufficient for sex-specific reference ranges, because the measured sex disparity is a discrimination gap that no threshold or rescaling can close, and local norms are the one repair the successor design has available.
