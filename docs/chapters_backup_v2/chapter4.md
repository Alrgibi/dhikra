# Chapter 4 — System Design

Chapter 3 described how the deployed model was fitted, evaluated and governed. This chapter describes the instrument built around it — the platform, the task battery and its stimuli, the report, and the boundaries at which the system refuses to produce anything — organised around the deployment constraint stated in Chapter 1: the instrument must remain usable where connectivity, hardware and specialist staffing cannot be assumed, and must therefore degrade gracefully, from a screening score, through an indicator profile computed without the trained model, down to a paper checklist requiring no computer. Section 4.3.1 examines the one assumption in the deployed pipeline that could not remain unexamined — the picture the system shows is not the picture the model was fitted on — section 4.5.1 states the property both of this chapter's defects shared — neither announced itself — and section 4.6 closes the chapter with a one-page summary of every verification the system as built received.

## 4.1 Architecture

The system is a pipeline from microphone to report, shown in Figure (4.1). A browser records the session; the server converts each recording to a sixteen-kilohertz mono waveform, extracts the acoustic measures and obtains a transcript; feature extraction turns the primary transcript into the model's input vector; the frozen screening model turns that vector into a screening score, a development-calibrated probability estimate; the risk-adjustment stage of section 3.7 turns that score into a context-adjusted estimated risk, conditioned on age and referral context and not clinically calibrated for Libya; and the report engine renders the result with its bands, reference ranges and caveats, all on one ordinary local computer. Automatic transcription is used when the recogniser's weights are present; when they are not — they must be downloaded once, which the target setting cannot assume — the platform does not fail and does not invent text but asks the operator to type the transcript, which is how the reference corpora themselves were produced. A missing network therefore costs convenience, never validity, and a missing model bundle still leaves a working platform that reports an indicator profile and never claims a screening score it cannot justify.

**Figure (4.1) : System architecture. The screening decision is computed from the picture description alone, the only task with healthy controls in the training corpora; story recall, verbal fluency and Qur'anic recitation contribute severity estimation and supporting context and never enter the screening decision; procedural discourse is recorded as connected-discourse material for the successor corpus and is not analysed.**

The deployed screening model is the frozen artefact of Chapter 3: a soft-voting ensemble of three tree learners — extremely randomised trees and a random forest of five hundred estimators each, and a gradient-boosting machine of one hundred and fifty depth-two estimators — each behind a median-imputation and standardisation pipeline, the ensemble wrapped in sigmoid calibration, reading 64 transcript-derived features at the operating threshold of 0.367 fixed before the external run. Tree ensembles over engineered features are the evidence-backed default for tabular problems of this size, and sigmoid rather than isotonic calibration is the appropriate choice below roughly a thousand samples [1, 2].

Figure (4.2) shows what feeds it. Dhikra extracts 117 speech and language measures across five families. Its externally evaluated English screening model uses a frozen 64-feature transcript-derived subset of linguistic, information-content and discourse-semantic measures; 53 acoustic measures support a separate language-independent pathway. Of the 117 measures extracted per session, the 64 language measures — 31 information-unit, 24 lexical and syntactic, 9 semantic-coherence — form the screening model's input, while the 53 acoustic measures feed a separate language-independent model that never enters the screening score. As section 3.3 records, the reduction to 64 is not a selection on performance but the set computable from a transcript alone — the property that makes the paper fallback examined in Chapter 5 possible.

**Figure (4.2) : Feature flow. Of the 117 measures extracted per session, the deployed screening model uses the 64 language features; the acoustic set supports a separate language-independent model and does not enter the screening score.**

## 4.2 The Assessment Platform

The platform is not operated by the elderly person alone. A family member, nurse or health worker opens the application, enters age, sex, education and the referral context, and hands the device over; the participant simply talks, and a complete session takes about six minutes. The battery, specified in section 3.5 and reordered on the evidence of section 5.25, is administered in the order of Table (4.1): connected discourse first, because it is the stronger genre for the mild-impairment target; picture description retained unchanged, because it alone has an externally validated model behind it; then verbal fluency and, in Arabic sessions, Qur'anic recitation from a twelve-surah bank restricted to what the participant is known to have memorised.

**Table (4.1) : The task battery in administration order, with each task's target and role**

| Order | Task | Target | Role in the session | Scored for screening |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Story recall | Mild impairment; episodic memory | An examiner-supplied story is read once and retold, so no cultural knowledge of any narrative is required; idea units recalled feed the severity index | No |
| 2 | Procedural discourse | Mild impairment | A step-by-step account of making tea, re-prompted once at most; needs no stimulus material at all, so it is the one task a paper fallback preserves intact; recorded as material for the successor corpus | No |
| 3 | Picture description | Dementia | The validated task: 64 features, the frozen model, the screening probability | **Yes** |
| 4 | Verbal fluency | Severity context | Animal names in sixty seconds, the standard battery form, reported against published norms | No |
| 5 | Qur'anic recitation | Overlearned memory; exploratory | Arabic sessions only; scored by length-normalised error against the surah actually recited | No |

Each task has a published basis, recorded so the battery is an argument rather than a habit: delayed story recall probes the earliest-declining domain and predicts progression from mild impairment to dementia, with the examiner-supplied story a project-authored instrument in that paradigm rather than a normed test [3]; sixty-second animal fluency is the standard batteries' own form, with age- and education-stratified norms [4, 5]; and the recitation probe rests on the documented survival of early-acquired, long-rehearsed material — musical memory is the strongest published analogue — while the task itself is validated nowhere, is framed as exploratory with six documented confounders, and its practical properties are design arguments, not evidence [6]. Procedural discourse alone entered on this project's own measurement, reported in section 5.25; its prompt asks for tea-making rather than the source corpus's sandwich because tea-making is culturally universal in the deployment setting.

The screening score nonetheless comes from picture description alone, and the code states the reason at the exact line where a different choice would be made, in a comment that is part of the submitted system: "the deployed model was trained and EXTERNALLY VALIDATED on picture description [...]. Scoring a task the model was never fitted for would forfeit that validation, which is the one property nothing else in this project has. The discourse transcripts are therefore COLLECTED (they are the material the Libyan MCI corpus needs [...]) but do not contribute to the screening score. Changing this line requires a new external validation, not a new argument." The report repeats the same fact to the operator in plain language, naming the discourse tasks as administered first, recorded, and contributing nothing to the result.

Repeat visits do not reuse material: selection from the three matched pictures, three twelve-proposition stories and the surah bank rotates deterministically from participant code and session number, so a repeat visit receives a different form while any session reproduces exactly from its recorded stimulus identifiers. The first English visit is the exception — the validated scene is shown by default, because rotating away from it would trade a calibrated result for protection against a practice effect that cannot yet exist. Fluency and story recall are shown to the operator as clinical context and excluded from the screening decision, because the training corpus contains them for the dementia group only, with no healthy controls from which a threshold could have been learned.

## 4.3 Stimuli

The three scenes of Figure (4.3) were drawn for this project, and the reason they were drawn rather than generated is metrological. A picture-description task is a measuring instrument: its scores are comparable only because every participant faces the same measured difficulty, which is why the field has kept one standard picture for decades — a different image each session would make the tool look more advanced while making its numbers meaningless. The correct response to repeat testing is parallel forms: matched stimuli drawn to one specification — three human figures, one animal, roughly fifteen nameable objects, eight describable actions, and exactly one hazard nobody in the scene has noticed, the element whose description requires reporting something implicit. The settings, dress and objects are Libyan rather than mid-century American, which the standard picture is: a participant who has never seen a cookie jar on a high shelf faces a harder task for reasons that have nothing to do with cognition [7].

**Figure (4.3) : The three picture-description stimuli, drawn for this project and matched on a content specification so that information-unit scoring is comparable across them. The kitchen scene is the only one against which the screening model is calibrated; the courtyard and market scenes exist so that a repeat assessment need not reuse a picture the participant has already described, and the system refuses to produce a score for either. The kitchen panel is the artwork as corrected when an audit of the frozen scoring key against the rendered scene found two scored units missing and three occluded; what the substitution still costs, and cannot stop costing, is measured in section 4.3.1.**

The kitchen scene is the only stimulus with a calibrated model behind it, and that sentence carries an assumption large enough to need its own section.

### 4.3.1 The Stimulus Is Not The One The Model Was Calibrated On And What That Costs


The Boston Diagnostic Aphasia Examination's Cookie Theft picture, the stimulus every training transcript describes, is not redistributable, so the deployed system shows a scene drawn for this project while the frozen scorer applies the Cookie Theft key — the 23 information units of the committed inventory — to descriptions of it. Substituting a stimulus while keeping a model fitted on the original was the single largest unexamined assumption in the deployed pipeline, and it was examined [7, 8, 9].

What inspection found is recorded first. Rendering the artwork and reading the key unit by unit showed that two of the 23 — the curtain and the dishcloth — were not depicted at all: no speaker, however intact, could earn them. Three more — the tap, the running water and the overflow — were drawn but occluded by the standing figure. Both faults were in the artwork; both were corrected the same day; and no participant data existed to be invalidated. Every figure in Chapters 3 and 5 was computed on transcripts of the real Cookie Theft and is unaffected by this defect.

The size of the omission was then measured — pre-registered, on the 548 Pittsburgh development recordings, perturbing only the inputs of the frozen model. Making the two absent units unearnable moves the mean probability by +0.0064, leaves sensitivity unchanged at 0.9869, and costs 2.88 percentage points of specificity (0.7119 to 0.6831), carrying seven recordings across the threshold. The registered grade is PRE-EDIT-DEFECT-CONTAINED, and it was contained for a checkable reason: the two units are rare even on the real picture, produced in 24.6% and 5.7% of recordings respectively (`results/stimulus_inventory_probe.json`).

What cannot be corrected is the other half. The scene contains salient content with no slot in the key — a cat, a teapot, a stove, bread, a table, a palm tree. The key cannot be extended to credit them, because the key computes the model's input and the model is frozen: adding a unit would change the information-unit features of every training transcript and void the calibration. The only possible effect of the unscored content is behavioural — attention spent naming what earns nothing is attention not spent naming what the scorer counts — and its magnitude on real speakers is unknowable without Libyan data. The pre-registered analysis therefore asked the inverse question: how many units would the substitution have to cost a speaker before the screening decision changes? Table (4.2) is the answer.

**Table (4.2) : Displaced information units against the fraction of control recordings crossing the screening threshold (548 Pittsburgh recordings, frozen model, forty replicates per row)**

| Units displaced | Mean change in probability | Control recordings crossing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | +0.0242 | **13.1%** |
| 2 | +0.0497 | 26.7% |
| 3 | +0.0741 | 38.8% |
| 4 | +0.0993 | 49.4% |
| 6 (pessimistic bound) | +0.1426 | 63.6% |

One displaced unit is enough to matter. Against a criterion fixed before execution — material if two displaced units move ten percent of controls across the threshold — the result graded DISPLACEMENT-MATERIAL from the first row. The result is in the direction the project did not want, and it is reported at full strength: whether the substituted picture costs real speakers anything is unknown, and cannot be known without Libyan data, which is why the pilot protocol carries a pre-specified stimulus-equivalence endpoint rather than a hope.

One diagnostic was declared post hoc and does not change the grade. The report already refuses a confident call within 0.1032 of the threshold, a band holding 45.3% of control recordings at baseline; 93.6% of the controls that cross at one displaced unit were already inside it, so the borderline caveat absorbs almost the whole small-displacement effect, leaving roughly two recordings per replicate moving from a confident negative to a positive. At six displaced units the protection fails — 46.1% — so the existing safeguard covers the likely case and not the worst one, which is why the substitution caveat of section 4.5 attaches to every score rather than to borderline ones.

One observation is recorded at exactly the weight it deserves: the author's impression, on viewing both scenes, is that this project's picture is harder to describe. That impression is n = 1, not blinded, and made by the person who commissioned the artwork — not evidence, and not written as evidence. It is recorded because it points the same way as the measurement, and because an impression written down before data arrive can be checked against them; the pilot's stimulus-equivalence endpoint is that check.

Finally, this section must not be conflated with the scorer-agreement check of section 5.5.1, which asks a different question of different data: it compares a hand count with the software count on transcripts of the real Cookie Theft, measuring the lexical matching rule rather than the picture. The two act on the same quantity in opposite directions — a hand count runs about 1.5 units above the software, a substituted picture may cost a speaker units — but the offsets are not designed to cancel, have never been measured together, and nothing may be inferred from their opposite signs.

## 4.4 The Report

What the operator receives is designed around the instrument's measured limits rather than around completeness. The screening result is a band with the screening score behind it, and an indicator profile in plain language — each measured family against reference ranges estimated from the training controls, so a family member can see what was unusual rather than only that something was. With age supplied, the risk-adjustment chain of section 3.7 converts the score into a context-adjusted estimated risk, not clinically calibrated for Libya, the referral multiplier declared in the output itself as a floor rather than an estimate. The severity index is reported as a band, never as a one-decimal estimate: its recorded accuracy could not be confirmed by the pre-registered reconstruction of section 5.7, the report says so where the band appears, and the point estimate survives only in an audit field no operator is shown. The recitation probe is scored against the surah actually recited rather than the one assigned — a participant who launches into a better-known passage would otherwise be scored as impaired for an administration slip — and where both recall and recitation exist the report contrasts new learning with overlearned memory.

## 4.5 Safety Boundaries

The system refuses to produce a result in four situations, each enforced in code rather than requested in documentation: a recording that fails the quality gate of Table (4.3); an Arabic session, for which no validated screening score exists — the indicator profile is computed by the Arabic engine, and no English reference range is ever applied to Arabic speech; a picture other than the calibrated kitchen scene, for which the trained model's screening score is withheld while the indicator profile stands; and any use outside the declared scope of the model card. The premise is stated in the quality module itself: a system that always returns a number is more dangerous than one that sometimes declines, because a score computed from ten seconds of mumbling in a noisy corridor is formatted exactly like a valid one.

**Table (4.3) : Quality-gate thresholds and the basis of each**

| Gate | Threshold | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Signal-to-noise ratio, refuse | below 7 dB on the estimator's scale | Empirical: clean recordings return a median of 11.1 dB on this estimator and the same files degraded to a true 10 dB — the measured point at which pause extraction fails outright — return 3.1 dB; the refusal sits between the two distributions |
| Signal-to-noise ratio, warn | below 10 dB | Empirical: the lower edge of the clean range |
| Clipping | above 5% of samples | Empirical: clipping measured harmless up to heavy levels, so the tolerance is deliberately loose |
| Duration | below 8 s | Asserted engineering minimum |
| Voiced speech | below 4 s | Asserted engineering minimum |
| Transcript length | below 8 words | Asserted engineering minimum |

The split in the basis column is deliberate. The noise thresholds and clipping tolerance derive from the degradation experiment of section 5.9 — the pipeline is robust to compression, an eight-kilohertz sampling rate, low volume and clipping, and broken by background noise — so the gate polices noise strictly and everything else loosely, the opposite of a generic audio check. The three minima were asserted, no experiment derives them, and the pilot's feasibility outcomes are the place to revisit them. A tool intended for a Libyan clinic must tolerate a cheap phone; it must not tolerate an open window onto a busy street.

Beyond refusing, the system confesses. Every screening score from the validated picture is delivered alongside the statement that the picture is a substitute, with the measured consequence of section 4.3.1 attached — the system has no evidence that would justify withholding the score, and no evidence that would justify presenting it as though the substitution were free, so it says which of the two situations it is in. The caveat is quoted in full, because it is submitted text — the deployed system saying aloud what this chapter measured:

> "The picture shown is not the picture this model was calibrated on. The original belongs to a published test and cannot be redistributed, so an equivalent scene drawn for this project is used instead. Every item the scorer looks for is present in it, but the two pictures are not interchangeable, and the effect of the substitution on real speakers has never been measured. If this picture costs a speaker even one of the items the original would have prompted, roughly one healthy recording in eight would move from a negative screen to a positive one. Weigh this score accordingly until the substitution has been checked on a local sample."

A second caveat appears whenever the word-vector model behind nine of the sixty-four features is absent from the machine, and it exists because of this chapter's second defect:

> "Nine of the sixty-four measurements this score is built from could not be computed on this computer, because the English word-meaning model is not installed. They have been replaced by the average value from the training data, which is the safest available substitute but is not a measurement of this person. Install it (python -m spacy download en_core_web_md) and re-analyse before relying on the score."

A third caveat names any score lying within one standard error of measurement of the threshold as sitting in the least stable region of the instrument, quoting the measured decision reproducibility of section 5.26. None of the three alters a score; each states what the score is.

### 4.5.1 Both Of This Chapter's Defects Were Silent

The stimulus was missing two of the twenty-three units the scorer looks for, and the one-click installer left nine of the model's sixty-four features filled with training averages on every computer it configured. Neither raised an error; both produced a number of exactly the expected type and range, and that is not coincidence — a median imputer is designed to substitute, a scorer is designed to return zero for an unmentioned unit, and at the interface a degraded value is indistinguishable from a legitimate one. The remedy is the one section 3.10 prescribes for registrations, extended to the deployed system: the startup banner states whether the vector model is present, and the report names each substitution when it occurs — the caveats quoted above are that remedy, running in the shipped code. The system is not more accurate for saying any of it; it is more honest, and the difference between those properties is what this chapter is about. The general form of the pattern — every defect this project found in its own analysis, artwork and deployment was silent until something outside the failing component was built to say so — belongs to the conclusions, and Chapter 6 states it.

## 4.6 Verification Summary

Table (4.4) collects on one page every verification activity the project performed on the system as built, with the method and the outcome of each. The sections and appendices named in the table own the detail; the table adds no result of its own, and it closes the chapter that describes the system so that a reader sees what was checked before reading what was measured.

**Table (4.4) : Verification summary — what was verified, how, and with what outcome**

| What was verified | How | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen-model reconstruction and the seven signatures | Architecture recovered by introspection of the deployed artefact under the pinned scikit-learn 1.8.0, no training script having been committed, and checked against seven pre-registered signatures of the locked outputs (section 3.9, Appendix C); the acoustic model's protocol confirmed by a pre-registered signature test (Appendix F) | 7 of 7 signatures matched; the acoustic model's embedded figure reproduces from a fresh fit by committed code |
| Quality-degradation testing | Eight controlled degradations applied to the recordings, each of nine acoustic measures correlated with its clean value (section 5.9) | Robust to compression, 8 kHz resampling, low volume, clipping and distance; pause measures degrade at 20 dB (worst correlation 0.59) and extraction fails at 10 dB, which sets the refusal threshold of Table (4.3) |
| Human information-unit scorer check | The automated scorer against a human rater on 20 blind transcripts, graded against a pre-registered criterion of mean absolute difference ≤ 1.200 (section 5.5.1) | 1.600, grade INADEQUATE, reported first; the disagreement is a near-constant undercount of about 1.5 units with rank order preserved (ICC 0.904), which is why the AUC results survive and why the pilot's scoring rule binds (Appendix B) |
| Visit-to-visit analysis | Controls at successive annual visits taken as the estimate of instrument noise (123 pairs, gaps up to 1.5 years), and the probability that a repeat recording lands on the same side of the threshold (sections 5.9.1 and 5.26.1) | Standard error of measurement 0.103 and minimal detectable change 0.286; decision reproducibility 0.8355, 0.669 within one standard error of the threshold and 0.954 outside it — the basis of the three-band report |
| Risk-layer property checks | The Bayesian chain evaluated over a 1,440-case grid — twelve ages, three contexts, five family-history states, eight speech scores — against nine registered properties (section 3.7, Appendix H) | 1,440 of 1,440 cases passed with zero failures; the relative-risk-as-odds defect at the oldest ages is disclosed, not patched (Appendix H) |
| Refusal-logic checks | Four refusal conditions enforced in code; the noise and clipping thresholds derived from the degradation experiment, the three minima asserted (section 4.5) | The passing path was exercised end to end by the demonstration session of Appendix E, every recording clearing the gate; the refusal branches themselves have no committed test, and that test is assigned to the pilot |
| Startup checks | At startup the platform reports whether the word-vector model behind nine of the sixty-four features is present, and every report names any feature substituted by a training average (section 4.5.1) | Implemented in the shipped code after the installer defect was found; the demonstration session's report carried the stimulus caveat only, so no feature was substituted in that run (Appendix E) |
| Locked external evaluation | One execution on the external corpus at the threshold fixed in advance, under the governance of section 3.9, then reproduced once under a protocol permitting one execution and no decision (sections 3.9.2 and 5.3) | AUC 0.8533 [0.7371, 0.9458], sensitivity 96.2%, specificity 33.3%; the reproduction matched to ten decimal places and cell for cell, and a tombstone file blocks any further run; the specificity is diagnosed in sections 5.15 to 5.19 |
